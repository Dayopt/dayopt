# 5 Practical Timeboxing Tips with Dayopt

To-do lists tell you _what_ to do. Timeboxing tells you _when_ and _for how long_. By placing each task into a specific time slot on your calendar, you transform vague intentions into concrete commitments. Here are five tips to make timeboxing work for you with Dayopt.

## 1. Start with Your Most Important Task

Don't fill your calendar from 9 AM onward. Instead, identify the one task that matters most today and timebox it first. In Dayopt, choose an activity to create a Plan and place it at the time when you have the most energy. Keep the specific task in its notes. See [planning and current shortcuts](/docs/plans).

The rest of your day builds around this anchor.

## 2. Use Realistic Durations

A common mistake is scheduling 4-hour blocks for focused work. Most people can sustain deep focus for 60-90 minutes at a time.

| Task Type        | Suggested Duration |
| ---------------- | ------------------ |
| Deep focus work  | 60-90 minutes      |
| Meetings         | 25 or 50 minutes   |
| Email / messages | 30 minutes         |
| Quick tasks      | 15 minutes         |

In Dayopt, drag the bottom edge of a Plan to resize it. You'll quickly develop a feel for how long things actually take.

## 3. Leave Buffer Time

Back-to-back timeboxes lead to burnout and schedule collapse. Leave 15-30 minutes between blocks for transitions, unexpected tasks, and mental breaks.

In Dayopt, check the empty space on your calendar before placing the next Plan. Leave room between activities rather than filling every gap.

## 4. Record What Happened

The magic of timeboxing isn't just planning — it's comparing your plan to reality. After an activity ends, use **"Record as is"** on its past Plan to copy the activity, times, and notes into an independent Record. Adjust the Record if the actual time differed. The Plan stays in place; editing or deleting either one does not change the other. See [recording time](/docs/records).

Over time, you'll notice patterns: tasks that always take longer than planned, times of day when you're most productive, and activities that consume more time than you realized.

## 5. Make Room for Routines

Decide when to do your regular activities, then place a Plan on each day you intend to do them:

- **Weekday mornings**: Planning session (15 min)
- **Every Friday**: Weekly review (30 min)
- **Daily**: Email triage (30 min)

Keep these times visible in your calendar and explicitly record the time you spend afterward.

## Getting Started

Timeboxing doesn't require perfection. Start with just 3-4 timeboxes per day. As the habit builds, you'll naturally expand to cover more of your schedule.

The key insight: **a partially followed timebox is still more effective than no plan at all.** Even when things don't go as planned, you'll learn something about how you spend your time.

Open Dayopt and choose an activity for your first Plan. Follow the [planning guide](/docs/plans) for the current steps and shortcuts.
